# Cover letter — spec

**Status:** v2. Companion to `SPEC.md` (§7, §9). One page by design — if it
grows, cut it back.

## The job

Show, in your restrained voice, that you read the listing and that your
background fits it. That's all. Not a sales pitch, not an enthusiasm contest. A
reader should finish thinking "he read this and can do the work," not "he
really wants this."

## Shape

Three short paragraphs, 250–350 words, signed.

1. **Your own framing.** Why the role fits you, plainly, in a sentence or two.
   Name the role and company so it's obviously not a form letter. That's the
   whole opening — no warm-up.
2. **The fit.** Take the two or three things the JD actually asks for and map
   real work to them: what you did, what happened. Pick your strongest matches
   and let them stand; don't answer every bullet. This is where "I read the
   listing" shows.
3. **Close.** One thing you'd want to talk through, or bring early. Then stop.

## Voice

Calibrated from your real letters in `voice/`, not from rules — the sound comes
from the examples. The only mechanical rules:

- Plain, declarative, past tense for finished work. One idea per sentence.
- No em-dashes (job-search register). Colons, semicolons, parentheses,
  periods; en-dashes for ranges.
- Concrete nouns over adjectives.

## Never

- **Quote the company's own words back at them.** No press releases, marketing
  copy, or mission-statement language. This was the v1 tell that read as
  bullshit. Permanent.
- **Company news as a hook** ("recently announced," "just launched"). Facts in
  the body must be durable, not timely.
- **"What excites me about / drew me to / pulled me to."**
- **Oversell.** A claim stays only if demonstrably true; otherwise state the
  plain fact. No "this sounds like a dream," no thanking them for reading.
- **Fog and filler:** "passionate about," "leveraging," "dynamic team,"
  "results-driven." If it could appear in anyone's letter, cut it.

## Grounding

Voice from `voice/`. Fit claims from `master-resume.md` (not in the master, not
in the letter). Company facts from the JD or durable knowledge — never
invented; the letter works on JD detail alone.

## Done when

- It would pass as written by you, and nothing in it could be lifted into a
  letter for another company.
- A reader can tell you read *this* listing.
- You change less than 10% before sending.
